SECTION 03

CAPABILITY, NOT ADJECTIVES.

Each block states what the concept does in one sentence a procurement officer could repeat. Nothing here claims a customer.

C-01

Event fusion

Carrier EDI, API pings, and AIS position deltas collapse into one deduplicated timeline. If two sources disagree, the timeline shows both — it never silently picks.

C-02

Exceptions with owners

Every exception opens as a task with a named desk and a clock. Silence is the bug; the design nags until someone marks it seen.

C-03

Commitment re-dating

When a leg slips, every promise downstream of it is recalculated and stamped with what changed and why — visible in the audit rail, not buried in email.

C-04

Party-scoped views

Shipper, carrier, and consignee see the same truth through different windows. A customer link shows status, not your margin.

C-05

Boring exports

Any view leaves as CSV or signed PDF with the raw event payloads attached. Reporting that can be cross-checked against source is reporting people trust.

C-06

Docker-scale docs

The concept's developer surface is spec-first: an OpenAPI sketch, webhook samples, and a status-code glossary readable without a sales call.

SECTION 06

FREQUENTLY IMPOSED QUESTIONS

Do you actually track freight?

We track design decisions. TrackLane is a studio concept build — the vessel “MV Sample”, the container, the lane, and the exception are all invented. The point of the piece is the information design: what a visibility product should put in front of a dispatcher, and in what order.

Why does the spec sheet use engineering-drawing formatting?

Because logistics software earns trust the same way freight equipment does: stated tolerances, revision numbers, and dated drawings. Listing "≤ 4s p95, design target" is more honest than a logo wall of carriers you haven't integrated with.

What's the orange dot on the map doing?

It's following the dashed route along a 9-second loop with SVG motion paths — no JavaScript. It exists to make the static page feel operational for a moment, and to stop for people who set reduced motion.

Is the exception event real?

Weather routing around an Atlantic waypoint in late March is a plausible scenario; this specific one is scripted. Real exception design is what's being demonstrated: re-dated commitments, an approval queue, and a notification draft a human confirms.

Could this be built for real?

That's the test we held ourselves to. Every screen here maps to data a mid-size 3PL could realistically assemble from carrier EDI 214/247 transactions, ocean carrier APIs, and AIS feeds. The hard parts — dedup, conflict surfacing, re-dating lineage — are named in the spec sheet rather than waved away.
